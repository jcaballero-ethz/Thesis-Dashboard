Characterizing and Interpreting Weather-Driven Stress Events in European Energy Systems

A framework to identify, rank, and characterize the most costly weather-driven periods in European energy systems, applied to both historical and future climate datasets across 60 climate scenarios and 27 countries.

Motivation

Renewable energy is weather-dependent. As Europe's power system shifts towards wind and solar, prolonged adverse weather — wind droughts, cold snaps, cloudy weeks — can strain the system simultaneously across entire regions. This thesis defines these as stress events and develops a framework to identify, rank, and characterize them.

Using the ZEN-garden optimization model across 60 climate scenarios and 27 European countries, applied to both historical and future climate datasets, the goal is to understand what drives the most severe and costly periods — and whether they follow recognizable patterns across climate conditions.

Cluster Climate

Wind CF, PV CF and heat demand anomalies per zone. Groups stress events by their meteorological signature across European zones.

Cluster Operational

Net transfer anomaly, storage level anomaly and storage rate anomaly per zone. Groups stress events by how the power system responded operationally.
